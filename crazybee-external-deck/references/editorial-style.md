# Editorial and storytelling style

## Communication standard

Write for the external audience. Every slide must make one claim, answer one likely question, or create one intentional emotional beat.

## Narrative pattern

Use the shortest arc that serves the situation:

1. Establish a relevant audience tension or opportunity.
2. Introduce Crazybee's point of view.
3. Show the product in use, not as an abstract feature list.
4. Prove value with approved evidence.
5. Explain the partnership, implementation, or next step.
6. Close with a specific invitation.

## Copy rules

- Prefer headlines that state the takeaway rather than label the topic.
- Keep body copy concise enough for presentation distance.
- Use plain commercial language; avoid inflated AI, innovation, ecosystem, disruption, and synergy claims unless supported.
- Pair features with outcomes: what users, merchants, partners, or institutions gain.
- Keep parallel grammar in lists.
- Use numerals for metrics and put definitions, dates, and sources nearby.
- Use speaker notes for detail that should not crowd the slide.
- For revisions of supplied decks, do not shorten source copy without explicit authorization. Add space, change composition, or split the page.

## Typography and spacing

- Use no editable audience-facing text smaller than 16 pt.
- Verify the rendered font. Do not trust a requested font-family name when the environment may substitute it.
- Prefer Poppins when available; use Avenir Next as the approved wider, rounder fallback when Poppins is unavailable.
- Give body paragraphs relaxed line spacing and visible separation from headings, numbers, and adjacent paragraphs.
- Keep number, label, and definition groups aligned and vertically separated.
- Avoid bordered card grids. Use flat grouping, whitespace, scale, and tonal contrast.
- Remove decorative red rules and long red bars when they do not convey meaning.

## Credibility stack

Increase trust through a sequence of verifiable elements:

- Real interface captures.
- Clear product workflow.
- Named and approved partners or customers.
- Dated metrics with definitions.
- Specific case outcomes.
- Authentic photography or documented deployment context.
- Transparent next steps and implementation boundaries.

Do not use logo walls, testimonials, or large numbers as decoration. Each proof element must support the slide's claim.
