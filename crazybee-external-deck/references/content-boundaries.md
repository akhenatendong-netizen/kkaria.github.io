# Content boundaries

## Never invent

- Revenue, growth, users, merchants, conversion, savings, engagement, retention, or geographic coverage.
- Customer, merchant, partner, university, investor, or government relationships.
- Testimonials, awards, certifications, security claims, or regulatory status.
- Product capabilities, integrations, availability, launch dates, or roadmap commitments.
- Pricing, contractual terms, or commercial guarantees.

## Required metadata for claims

Where relevant, record the source, owner, measurement period, definition, approval status, and last-updated date.

## Placeholders

Use visible, specific placeholders such as `[Approved merchant count — owner: Sales]`. Never create plausible dummy numbers. Surface every unresolved placeholder at handoff.

## Supplied-deck content

- Preserve all audience-facing source words, numbers, qualifiers, labels, and source lines unless the user explicitly authorizes a change.
- Do not trade content fidelity for visual simplicity. Redesign or add slides instead.
- Treat source-raster text as content, including competitive tables, closing claims, and labels embedded in images.
- Remove production annotations only when their role as internal instructions is clear and their requested change has been implemented.

## Confidentiality

- Default to omitting unapproved client names, internal financials, personal data, unpublished roadmap items, and confidential screenshots.
- Do not embed credentials, analytics exports containing personal data, or private annotations in assets or speaker notes.
- Ask for explicit approval before using sensitive facts in an external deck.

## Rights

Use only approved logos, fonts, photography, screenshots, and mockup assets. Record licenses or provenance when external assets are introduced.
